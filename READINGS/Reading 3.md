the text emphasizes that in order to pinpoint some things right. You can create different levels of prototypes in order to not be distracted by unnecessary informations. An example provided is the 2D top down view of a house and it's 3D virtual model. Both can give very different informations and inspecting either of them but in a separated manner can giver clearer interpretations of the good and bad aspects of the project.

There is also this part of the text in which a prototype of a moblie phone is shown to people but in three different levels of prototypes. There is a rough paper skecth of the phone, a computer simulated version and a workable mobile phone. The results showed that people were having troubles in certains aspects of the protoype but these aspects only appeared because the prototyped was not defined clearly enough. There were also real problems that were not found with the two less defined prototypes because they lacked the physicality of the actual mobile phone.

This shows that in order to collect the amount of feedback you want to get from the prototype you create, you need to define the prototype as much as you want feedback from users so that they do not get confused with things that were not defined enough in the prototype. It may have been clear in your mind that X thing works or will work like that in the final product but your tester does not know that because it's not there in the prototype or was very vague. They are not in your shoes and my not get the full scope of the project. It's your job to guide them by filtering the data you want to extract from them.

You have known unkwons and unknowns unknowns. There are so much stuff that you have to keep in mind when creating a project and a prototype for it that you can't expect to know everything from the begining. It's useful to define it as much as possible but you have to let things be discovered during th creation process. You just have to react and adapt with a clear mind.

In the digital space I start with an idea that I cam in my mind or maybe from a picture I found online. I will start by adding rough shapes and model them into shapes that look progressively closer to the idea in mind. Sometimes I will accidently create wierd shapes that I did not expect at first. Most of the time, I have a rough draft or idea of the final product in my mind and it's through those many experimentations with the shapes i generate that I end up defining more and more the final result of the model. For example, I want to create some kind of robot head with a particular shape. I do the general shape and then through those shape experimentation I start to had gadgets and ornaments to give style and also function. Maybe I will had a gaz mask or tubes at the end but at first I didn't not know I wanted to do that.
